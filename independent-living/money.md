# Money

> The goal is not to become a finance wizard. The goal is to avoid preventable money chaos.

## Quick Answer

- Pay required expenses first.
- Move savings before spending money casually.
- Use credit cards like debit cards.
- Never carry a balance because someone told you it helps build credit.
- Review subscriptions regularly.

## Basic Monthly Budget

Money comes in, required expenses get paid, savings gets moved, and what remains is spending money.


## Checking and Savings

**Checking** is for money that moves soon: rent, food, transit, bills.

**Savings** is for money that should not disappear casually: emergencies, travel, future costs.

Important terms:

- **Available balance:** what the bank says can be used now.
- **Pending transaction:** a charge that has not fully posted yet.
- **Overdraft:** spending more than the account has, often with fees.

Account balance is not always the same as money available to spend. Pending charges can make the number lie a little.

## Credit Cards

**Best rule:** use a credit card like a debit card.

Only charge something if the money already exists in checking.

Set autopay to:

> **Statement balance, in full, every month.**

Know the basics:

- **Debit card:** pulls money from checking.
- **Credit card:** borrows money until the bill is paid.
- **Statement balance:** what you owe for the billing cycle.
- **Minimum payment:** the smallest payment that avoids being late, but interest can still grow.
- **Interest:** extra money charged when a balance carries over.
- **Utilization:** how much of your available credit you use; lower is usually better.

## Emergency Fund

Even a small reserve matters.

Milestones:

- first $100,
- then $500,
- eventually $1,000 or more.

The point is breathing room, not perfection.

## Scams

Be suspicious of:

- Venmo or Zelle requests from people you do not know,
- fake job offers,
- fake apartment listings,
- phishing emails,
- gift-card payment demands,
- urgent requests for money.

If someone demands secrecy, speed, or gift cards, slow down.

## Financial Aid Refunds

A refund is not automatically free money.

Some of it may come from loans or aid meant to cover semester expenses. Before spending it, ask what it needs to pay for later.

## Subscriptions and Discounts

Small recurring charges add up quietly.

- [ ] Review subscriptions monthly or quarterly.
- [ ] Cancel things you are not using.
- [ ] Look for student discounts.
- [ ] Use free campus resources included in tuition before paying elsewhere.

## Campus Jobs and Taxes

Campus jobs are usually W-2 employment.

Basic notes:

- taxes may be withheld from paychecks,
- tax documents usually arrive after the year ends,
- keep W-2s and other tax forms,
- ask Payroll or Student Employment when confused.

This is not tax advice. It is a reminder not to throw away tax documents.

## Affordability

> "Can I buy it?" and "Can I afford it?" are different questions.

If buying it makes the next two weeks stressful, it may not be affordable.

# Graduate School

> This page is for early exploration, not pressure. A PhD path is built over time.

## Quick Answer

- Graduate school planning can start early, but it does not need to be fully decided in year one.
- Strong math preparation usually means coursework, research exposure, faculty relationships, and proof that Jasper can work independently.
- Recommendation-letter relationships matter.
- Funding, advisor fit, and research area matter more than school name alone.

## What to Do Now

- [ ] Ask math faculty what preparation matters most.
- [ ] Keep a list of interesting math topics.
- [ ] Try talks, seminars, or reading groups when available.
- [ ] Look for undergraduate research or independent study options.
- [ ] Save project notes for future applications.

## National Resources

- [NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program) — Major fellowship for students entering or beginning research-based STEM graduate programs.
- [MathJobs.org](https://www.mathjobs.org/jobs) — Mostly useful later, during graduate school and academic job searches.
- [NSF Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Research Fellowships](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/msprf-mathematical-sciences-postdoctoral-research-fellowships) — Long-term reference, not an undergraduate priority.

## Questions to Ask Faculty

- How does a student identify a research area?
- What undergraduate courses matter most for graduate preparation?
- When should Jasper look for research experience?
- What makes a PhD advisor a good fit?
- When is a master's degree useful before a PhD?
